Building a Personal Fact Graph

What it is

Self-knowledge is scattered. Facts about your life live in journals, notes, memory — disconnected, with no structure to show how they actually relate to each other. The more you accumulate, the harder it is to see the whole.

I built a graph that turns plain text facts into an interactive visualization. Each fact gets enriched with context and placed near the facts it's most related to. The output is a map that makes the hidden web visible.

How the pipeline works

  new fact (plain text)
        |
        v
  web search for context (Tavily)
        |
        v
  summarize fact + context (GPT-4o-mini)
        |
        v
  embed summary (text-embedding-3-small)
        |
        v
  build mutual k-NN graph over embeddings
        |
        v
  Louvain community detection
        |
        v
  labeled clusters, rendered as a graph

Getting the clustering right

This part took a few tries.

  • HDBSCAN was the first attempt. It left about a third of items unclustered as noise, which defeats the point of a graph meant to hold everything.
  • Agglomerative clustering with silhouette-based K selection was next. Silhouette scores kept favoring implausible K values, like K=15 for a set of 30 facts. The metric was optimizing for tight, well-separated blobs, which isn't how personal facts are actually distributed.
  • The fix was to stop treating this as a geometric clustering problem. Personal facts don't sit in clean separable clusters — they sit in a web of overlapping, weaker connections. Building a mutual k-NN graph and running Louvain community detection on it matches that structure instead of fighting it.

Two other details mattered:

  • The mean pairwise cosine similarity across facts was much lower than expected, something like 0.18. A similarity threshold tuned for a domain like news articles (0.50) was far too strict and collapsed a graph with nearly 200 mutual edges down to 6. Dropping the threshold to around 0.30 restored the graph.
  • Louvain's resolution parameter needed manual tuning to avoid both a handful of giant clusters and dozens of tiny ones.
  • The labeling prompt explicitly forbids generic names like "Overview" or "Miscellaneous," which pushed the model toward specific, descriptive cluster names instead of defaulting to filler categories.

What's next

  • Testing alternative community detection methods (Leiden in particular) to see if it gives more stable clusters across reruns, since Louvain's output can shift slightly depending on node ordering.
  • Looking into temporal decay, so older facts that are less relevant to who I am now have less pull on the graph's structure.
  • Exploring whether edge weights should incorporate more than cosine similarity — possibly blending in recency or source confidence from the web search step.
  • Considering a lightweight way to re-cluster incrementally as new facts come in, rather than rerunning the full pipeline each time.
  • Thinking about whether cluster labels should evolve over time as a community's contents shift, instead of being fixed at creation.
